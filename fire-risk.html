The fire risk

One vape in the wrong bin can start a fire.

Every vape has a lithium-ion battery sealed inside. Put it in a household bin and it gets crushed in the truck, then again at the facility. That’s how loads, trucks and whole recycling plants catch fire.

How a vape fire starts

From the kitchen bin to a burning load.

A “finished” vape usually still holds charge. Nothing happens until the battery is damaged, and the waste system is designed to crush things.

  1. Thrown in a bin

    Into rubbish, recycling or a street bin, often by people who don’t know there’s a battery inside.3

  2. Compacted in the truck

    The truck’s compactor presses the load, bending and puncturing what’s inside.

  3. Crushed again at the facility

    Tipped, pushed by machines, shredded or baled. Each step is another chance to damage the cell.

  4. The battery overheats

    A damaged lithium cell can heat itself uncontrollably and burst into flame, sometimes long after the damage.

  5. The load catches fire

    Surrounded by paper, cardboard and plastic, a small battery fire becomes a truck or facility fire.

Case: Canberra, December 2022

A whole recycling plant written off.

Fire tore through the ACT’s main recycling facility at Hume. The building was declared a structural write-off and Canberra’s recycling had to be sent elsewhere while a replacement was built. Investigators looked at lithium batteries, and vaping products were not ruled out.4

When EPA Victoria later spoke to the Victorian Parliament about vape waste, this was the most significant e-cigarette fire it cited.3

It’s already illegal to landfill vapes in Victoria

Since 2019, it has been illegal in Victoria to put e-waste in household rubbish or landfill. E-waste means anything with a plug, battery or power cord, and the state government lists e-cigarettes as e-waste.7

Not every e-waste point can take vapes

EPA Victoria told Parliament that while every Victorian lives within a 30-minute drive of an e-waste facility, not all of them can manage vapes, and many people don’t realise vapes contain batteries at all.3 That’s the gap a dedicated vape service fills.

Where vapes cause fires

  • Rubbish, recycling and green bins
  • Public litter bins
  • Loose in a drawer, glovebox or box of confiscated vapes
  • Cut open at home to “get the battery out”

Where they should go

  • A dedicated vape collection bin
  • Drop-off at our North Geelong facility
  • Kept whole, cool and lidded until then
  • Damaged ones isolated in metal or sand
