How we recycle

Recycling a vape is a chain. Every link has to hold.

A vape is a battery, a circuit, metal and plastic, sealed together around a nicotine liquid. Getting those apart safely takes the right collection, storage, transport and processing. Skip a step and it becomes a fire or a pollution risk.

What’s inside

Small device. Six different materials.

That mix is exactly why a vape can’t go through kerbside recycling. Sorting lines are built for clean, single materials like paper, glass and rigid plastic, not sealed devices with a live battery inside.

Why it has to be done properly

The shortcuts that go wrong.

Each of these feels like the easy option. Each one moves the risk somewhere else: into a truck, a facility, a drawer or the ground.

“It’s small, it can go in the bin”

The bin goes into a compactor truck, then a facility full of paper and plastic. That’s where battery fires start. In Victoria it’s also against the law. See the fire risk

“I’ll cut it open and pull the battery out”

Disposable vapes aren’t built to be opened. Cutting or prying the casing can puncture the cell and make it spark or catch fire in your hands.

“We’ll keep them in a box for now”

A box of confiscated vapes is a box of charged batteries touching each other. Stored loose, warm or for months, the risk grows with every one you add.

“Landfill is fine for a few”

Batteries can still ignite once buried, and residues from e-liquid and electronics can leach into soil and water. The recoverable materials are lost for good.

“Any e-waste point will do”

Not all e-waste facilities can manage vapes, as EPA Victoria told the state Parliament.3 Check before you drop them anywhere.

“Leave it on the bench”

E-liquid can contain nicotine. Over 2009–16, 38% of calls to Australian Poisons Information Centres about e-cigarettes involved children.*

Done properly

From your counter to recovered material.

This is the route every vape we receive takes, whether it comes from a collection bin or over the counter at North Geelong.

  1. Collected separately

    In dedicated vape bins or at our drop-off, never mixed into general or recycling waste.

  2. Stored safely

    Lidded, cool and dry. Damaged, swollen or leaking units are isolated from the rest.

  3. Moved as battery waste

    Full bins are swapped for empty ones and transported as battery waste, not as rubbish.

  4. Made safe & separated

    Batteries are separated from casings, and metals, plastics and electronics are split into their own streams.

  5. Materials recovered

    Each stream goes to recyclers that can turn it back into usable material, keeping it out of landfill.

Vapes “can cause fires when they are not sorted, separated, handled, or stored appropriately.”

EPA Victoria, evidence to the Victorian Parliament, July 2024
